# Reel Ledger — manual

Reel Ledger is a phone app for ticking off the films and TV shows you've seen and the games you've played.

**Movies**
- **IMDb Top 250** — the chart as of September 2026 (ranks, ratings, vote counts).
- **Best Picture** — every nominee and winner for the Academy Award for Best Picture, 1st ceremony (1929) through 98th (March 2026): 621 films, 98 winners.

**TV**
- **IMDb Top 250 TV** — the TV chart as of September 2026.
- **Emmys** — every winner and nominee of the Primetime Emmy for Outstanding Drama Series (1951–2026), Outstanding Comedy Series (1952–2026) and Outstanding Limited or Anthology Series (1973–2026). This is TV's Best Picture.

**Games**
- **Top 250 games** — ranked by player ratings on [Backloggd](https://backloggd.com) (the Letterboxd of games), counting only games that a lot of people have played, the same idea as IMDb's minimum number of votes.
- **Game of the Year** — every winner and nominee of the D.I.C.E. Awards' Game of the Year (the industry academy's award, games' Best Picture), with The Game Awards and BAFTA as second opinions.

That's 778 films, 741 shows and 416 games, plus 1,441 recommended films, 1,124 recommended shows and 637 recommended games that aren't on any list. You can add your own lists of books, games or anything else.

## Movies, TV, Games or Mix

The switch at the top (**Movies · TV · Games · Mix**) decides what you're looking at. Each is a separate list with its own progress, tabs and recommendations.

- **Movies**: the tabs are All, Top 250, **Oscars**, For you, Lists.
- **TV**: the same tabs, with **Emmys** in place of Oscars and the Top 250 TV chart.
- **Games**: the same tabs, with **GOTY** (Game of the Year) in place of Oscars and the Top 250 games.
- **Mix**: several at once. Tap **Mix**, then tap Movies, TV or Games to switch each one on or off (at least one stays on). Every poster gets a small **Film**, **TV** or **Game** tag. Top 250 and Awards get a switch at the top to pick which list. Use Mix to search a person or company across media (a composer who scored films and games, a studio that made both), or to get For you picks from everything.

## The five tabs

| Tab | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **All** | Every title in one poster grid. Your progress on the three built-in lists sits at the top. |
| **Top 250** | The IMDb chart in rank order. Each poster shows its rank. |
| **Oscars / Emmys** | One block per ceremony, newest first: the winner large, the other nominees in a row you swipe sideways. "Winners only" hides the nominees. Each block shows how many of that year's race you've seen (e.g. 6/10). On Emmys, chips pick which categories to show: Drama, Comedy, Limited. |
| **For you** | Recommendations gathered from every film you've marked seen, ranked by hits. See below. |
| **Lists** | Your lists, new lists, backups, and install help. |

## Marking films

- **Tap the square on a poster** to mark it seen. Tap again to unmark. A seen poster dims and the square turns green.
- **Tap the poster or title** to open its detail card.
- In the detail card, **Mark seen** and **Want to see** are the two big buttons. "Want to see" puts an orange bookmark on the poster and adds the film to your watchlist.
- Marking a film seen removes it from the watchlist automatically.

## TV shows: seasons, format and status

**Season by season.** Shows with more than one season have a row of season buttons (S1, S2, S3…) in their detail card. Tap the ones you've watched. A partly watched show gets a green progress bar on its poster and a badge like *3/8*, and appears under the **Watching** chip. Mark every season (or tap **Mark seen**) and it counts as seen.

**Format** (filter: *TV format*):
| Format | Means | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Miniseries | One complete story in a single season (limited series) | Chernobyl, Band of Brothers |
| Serialized | One continuing story across seasons | Breaking Bad, The Sopranos |
| Semi-serialized | Stand-alone episodes plus season-long arcs | The X-Files, Buffy |
| Procedural | A case, patient or crime of the week | CSI, House, NCIS |
| Sitcom | Situation comedy | Seinfeld, The Office |
| Anthology | New story and cast each season or episode | True Detective, Fargo, Black Mirror |
| Docuseries | Documentary series | Planet Earth |
| Sketch / variety | Sketch, talk or variety show | Saturday Night Live |
| TV film / Special | A single TV movie or one-off program (a few Emmy nominees) | |

**Status** (filter: *TV status*):
- **Ongoing**: still airing or renewed (as of October 2026).
- **Concluded**: ended as its creators planned. A finished miniseries shows as *Complete*.
- **Cancelled**: ended by the network or streamer before the planned end. The card says whether it *wrapped up its story* or left it **unresolved**.
- **Unresolved ending**: a separate filter for shows that ended on a cliffhanger or with major storylines open.

For example, *TV format: Miniseries* + *For you* lists only miniseries recommended from what you've watched. *TV status: Concluded* hides shows you'd get stuck on.

**Awards on a show's card:** its Emmy races (tap a year chip to see that year's full race, with checkboxes), its Emmy record for Outstanding Series, and its Golden Globe record for Best Series.

## Video games

**Your progress has three levels** instead of seen / not seen:

| Level | Means | On the poster |
|---|---|---|
| **Played** | Started it, or played some of it | Half-filled green square, counts as *in progress* |
| **Finished** | Beat the main story or campaign | Green check, counts as done |
| **100%** | Fully completed: everything, every ending or trophy you care about | Green check plus a gold **100%** badge |

- **Tap the square on a poster** to step up a level: none → Played → Finished → 100% → none. A small message confirms the new level.
- In the detail card, the three buttons **Played · Finished · 100%** set the level directly; tap the active one again to clear it.
- Status chips in Games: All · Unplayed · Played · Finished · 100% · Want to play.

**Each game's card shows:**
- Developer, publisher, director, writers, composer and main cast (all tappable, like film people)
- **Series**: tap it to see every game in that franchise in order
- **Platforms**: every console or PC it came out on, with the year of each port (original platforms first)
- **Versions**: remasters, remakes and definitive editions, with year and platforms, plus *Remake of…* when the game is itself a remake
- **Players**: single-player, how many on one console, how many online, co-op (local or online), split-screen, competitive, crossplay, and a warning when the **online servers are shut down**
- **Length**: hours for the main story, main + extras, and 100% (HowLongToBeat-style estimates)
- **Play it today**: the easiest way to play it now (a remaster, a Switch Online app, original hardware…)
- Setting, true story, historical, based on, original language, DLC, its Game of the Year record and races, and *More like this*
- Links to Backloggd, HowLongToBeat and Wikipedia

**Filters for games** (Filters → *Platform*, *Story* and the game sections):
- **Platform**: PS5, PS4, Switch, Xbox Series, PC… down to NES and Dreamcast. A game counts on every platform it was released on, including later ports.
- **Play style**: Co-op, Couch co-op / local multiplayer, Split-screen, Online multiplayer (only where servers still run), Single-player only, Open world
- **Versions**: Has a remaster or remake, Is a remake
- **Length**: Short (10 h or less), Long (40 h or more); sort by **Shortest to beat**
- **On current platforms**: playable on PS5, Xbox Series, Switch, Switch 2 or PC
- **Free-to-play, live-service and mobile games are hidden** unless you turn on *Include free-to-play, live-service and mobile games*. Only a handful exist here anyway (award nominees like Hearthstone or Pokémon Go); the Top 250 leaves them out.
- Sort by **Most GOTY wins**, rating, rank, newest, oldest…

Example: *Games* + *Couch co-op* + *Split-screen* + *On current platforms* gives a list for a game night on the couch.

**GOTY tab**: one block per year, newest first, with chips to show **D.I.C.E.**, **The Game Awards** and **BAFTA** races (any combination). Award years are the year the games came out (the 2025 games were honored at ceremonies in December 2025 – April 2026). The counter shows how many of that year's nominees you've finished.

## Studios, distributors and networks

Every film shows its main **production companies** and its **distributor** (the company that first released it in cinemas). Every show shows its **network** (where it first aired) and its **studio**. Every game shows its **developer** (the studio that made it) and **publisher** (the company that released it). All of these are tappable, like people's names: tapping *Warner Bros.* or *HBO* lists everything they made or released in the app, with how many you've seen. The small gold number next to a company tells you how many titles it has.

Filters also has a **Company** section with the 30 biggest names for what you're viewing.

## View-only lock

Hand your phone to someone, or just browse, without changing anything:

- **Tap the lock** (top right) to switch to view only. It turns gold and the header says *View only*.
- While locked, unseen posters have no checkbox, so tapping anywhere on a poster opens its details. Seen films keep their green check.
- Mark seen, Want to see, season buttons, race checkboxes, restoring a backup and editing lists are all blocked. Search, filters, sorting and details work as usual.
- **To unlock, press and hold the lock for about a second** until the green ring closes. A quick tap won't unlock it.
- The lock stays on after you close the app.

## The detail card

Each film shows:

- Plot summary, director, writers, main cast
- Release year and **setting**: when and where the story takes place, with a note when it's set in its own time
- **True story** (yes/no), **Historical / period piece** (yes/no), and what it's based on (novel, play, true story…)
- Runtime, country, language, IMDb rank and rating
- **Oscar record**: every Oscar it won (gold dot) and every nomination it lost (hollow dot)
- For any Best Picture nominee: **the full Best Picture race of that year**, winner first, each with its own checkbox. Tap a name to jump to that film; tap its box to mark it without leaving.
- **More like this**: six similar films with a one-line reason each. Some are on your lists, others aren't (marked **Off list**). Tap one to open it; the **‹** button at the top of the card takes you back.
- Links to IMDb, Wikipedia, and JustWatch (where to stream it)

Tap outside it, tap ✕, or press your phone's Back button to close it.

## People

Every director, writer and cast name on a detail card can be tapped. It opens that person's **filmography in the app**: every film they directed, wrote or acted in across your lists and the recommended picks, oldest first, with how many you've seen. The small gold number next to a name tells you how many titles they have in the app. All filters still work on top (for example *Not seen* to see which of a director's films you're missing). Tap ✕ on the person card to go back to everything.

## Off-list films

Recommendations include 1,441 films that aren't on the IMDb Top 250 or Best Picture lists, and 1,124 shows that aren't on the TV lists. They get the same full detail card as every other film (plot, director, writers, cast, setting, true story, historical, based on), plus a **Recommended from** list showing which of your films pointed to them and why. The 703 that won or were nominated for Oscars in any category also show their Oscar record and link straight to their IMDb page; the rest open an IMDb search. You can mark them seen or want to see like any other film; once marked they also appear in **All**. To browse them all, use Filters → In list → **Recommended films** or **Recommended shows**.

## For you

The **For you** tab looks at every film you've marked seen, collects their *More like this* picks, and ranks them by **hits**: how many of your seen films recommend each one. A film with *4 hits* is recommended by four films you've watched, so it's the safest bet.

- It shows films you haven't seen yet. The **Seen** chip shows the ones you'd already watched, and **Want to see** shows the ones on your watchlist.
- Mark a suggestion seen and it leaves the list next time you open the tab. Its own recommendations join in, so the list keeps learning from what you watch.
- Filters and search work here too: e.g. *Not in English* + *2000s*, or *Recommended films* / *Recommended shows* to see only discoveries outside your lists.

## Search, status and filters

- **Search** matches title, director, writer and cast. Try "Kubrick" or "Meryl Streep".
- **Status chips** under the search bar: All · Not seen · Seen · Want to see. Each shows a live count.
- **Filters** opens a panel with:
  - **Sort**: best for this view, IMDb rank, IMDb rating, newest, oldest, title, most Oscar wins, recently marked seen
  - **In list**: IMDb Top 250, Best Picture winners, Best Picture nominees, and your own lists
  - **Story**: based on a true story, historical/period, based on a book, animated, not in English, under 100 min, over 150 min, before 1960, 2020 or later, 3+ Oscar wins
  - **Genre** and **Decade**
- Filters combine. Example: *Not seen* + *Best Picture winners* + *Based on a true story* + sort *Oldest first* gives a history-film watchlist in order.
- "Clear filters" above the grid resets everything.

## Your own lists (series, books, games…)

In the **Lists** tab, under **New list**:

1. Type a name, choose a kind (Movies, Series, Books, Games, Other), tap **Create list**.
2. On the new list, tap **Add a title**: title, year, creator (director / author / studio), optional note.
3. Titles you add get the same seen / want-to-see marks and show up in All, Search and Filters.

**Pasting a whole list at once.** Tap **Paste a list file** and paste JSON in this shape:

```json
{
  "list": { "name": "Best video games", "media": "game" },
  "items": [
    { "title": "Hollow Knight", "year": 2017, "creator": "Team Cherry",
      "plot": "A knight explores a ruined insect kingdom.", "genres": ["Adventure"],
      "poster": "https://… (optional image URL)" }
  ]
}
```

`media` can be `movie`, `series`, `book`, `game` or `other`. For movies you can also give `imdb`, `directors`, `writers`, `cast`, `runtime`, `trueStory`, `historical`, `basedOn`, `source` and `setting`. If a movie's `imdb` ID is already in the app, the existing entry is reused, so your marks carry over.

The easy way to get such a file: ask Claude, *"Make a Reel Ledger list file of the top 100 TV series"*, then paste the result.

## Backups

Your marks live on the phone, in this app only. To keep them safe or move them to another device:

- **Copy backup** puts all your marks and custom lists on the clipboard. Paste it into a note, email or your tracker.
- **Restore from backup** → paste → **Restore**. It merges, so nothing already marked is lost.
- On the installed app there's also **Download backup file**.

## Install on your phone

Reel Ledger is a web app you install from the browser. It needs to be hosted at an `https://` address once; GitHub Pages does this for free.

**Host it (once, ~5 minutes):**

1. On github.com, create a new **public** repository, e.g. `reel-ledger`.
2. Upload every file from the `reel-ledger` folder (keep `data/catalog.json` inside its `data` folder, and include the `.nojekyll` file).
3. Repository **Settings → Pages** → Source: *Deploy from a branch*, Branch: `main`, folder `/ (root)` → Save.
4. After a minute the app is live at `https://<your-username>.github.io/reel-ledger/`.

**Install (Android, Chrome):** open that address → menu ⋮ → **Install app** (or *Add to Home screen*). It opens full screen, has its own icon, and works offline.

**iPhone (Safari):** Share → **Add to Home Screen**.

**Preview without hosting:** a copy also runs as a page inside Claude (link on the tracker item). It works the same, but it can't be installed, shows title cards instead of posters, and keeps its own marks. Use Copy backup / Restore to move marks to the installed app.

Game covers come from Wikipedia, like film posters.

Posters download the first time you open the app with internet, then stay cached for offline use. Film posters come from Wikipedia. TV posters come from TVmaze, which allows about 20 lookups every 10 seconds, so the first time you open TV the posters on screen load first and the rest (about 1,800 shows) fill in over several minutes in the background. Titles without a poster show a colored title card; where only a wide logo exists it's centered on a light card.

## Updating the data later

- All films live in `data/catalog.json`. To refresh the IMDb chart or add next year's Oscar race, replace that file and upload it to the repository; the app picks it up on next open. Your marks are kept because they're stored by IMDb ID.
- To add a new kind of media to the app itself, add one line to the `MEDIA` table at the top of the script in `index.html` (label, plural, what to call the creator).

## Sources and accuracy

- **IMDb Top 250**: chart snapshot from September 2026. The live chart moves a few places every week.
- **Emmy and Golden Globe series awards**: the winners and nominees tables on Wikipedia's pages for each category, current through the September 2026 Emmys (78th) and the January 2026 Golden Globes. The 2026 Emmy comedy winner (*Widow's Bay*) was confirmed from news reports. Early Emmy years where a category was split or combined keep the main series category only.
- **TV details** (creators, cast, network, studio, seasons, format, status) and **film studios and distributors**: written for this app from general knowledge, the same way as the film details.
- **Oscar nominations and wins**: the Academy's records, via the open `oscar_data` dataset on GitHub, current through the 98th Academy Awards. Director names were cross-checked against the Academy's Best Director nominations; none conflicted.
- **Plot, cast, writers, setting, true story and historical flags**: written for this app from general film knowledge. They're right for the vast majority of titles; 56 titles (mostly 1930s nominees and 2025–26 releases) carry a note on their card saying the details are less certain. If something looks off, the IMDb link is one tap away.
- **Recommendations**: six hand-picked similar films per title, chosen for this app from general film knowledge (not an algorithm on viewing data). Off-list films' details were written the same way; their Oscar records and IMDb links come from the Academy's data where the film was nominated.
- **Top 250 games**: Backloggd's *Top rated* list (player averages out of 5), fetched October 31, 2026, keeping only games among Backloggd's roughly 1,500 most-played. Without that cutoff the raw list is topped by fan games, obscure visual novels and games a few hundred people rated. Editions and remasters count once under the original game (e.g. *Bloodborne: Game of the Year Edition* → *Bloodborne*); true remakes count separately (*Resident Evil 4* 2005 and 2023). DLC, compilations, mods, free-to-play, live-service and mobile games were left out. Backloggd refused 5 of the popularity pages, so about 60 clearly mainstream games from those gaps (Chrono Trigger, Astro Bot, Final Fantasy VII Rebirth…) were let back in by hand.
- **Game of the Year awards**: the winners and nominees tables on Wikipedia for the D.I.C.E. Awards (from 1998), The Game Awards (from 2014) and the BAFTA Games Awards (from 2004), current through the 2025 games.
- **Game details** (platforms, versions, players, length, people): written for this app from general knowledge and checked against the web for recent and obscure games; about a quarter are marked medium confidence. Hours are estimates in the style of HowLongToBeat.
- **Posters**: films use the lead image of their English Wikipedia article. TV shows use posters from [TVmaze](https://www.tvmaze.com) (data licensed CC BY-SA; each show's card links to its TVmaze page), with Wikipedia as a backup.
